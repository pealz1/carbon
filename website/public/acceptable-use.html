Acceptable Use Policy

Last updated November 24, 2026

The short version: listen to music you’re entitled to, be decent to people, and don’t attack, overload or scrape the Services. Shared playlists, profiles and room names must be legal and must not harass anyone. We remove content and suspend accounts that break these rules, and we report child sexual abuse material to the authorities. This policy is part of the Terms of Service.

About this policy

This policy explains what you may and may not do with Carbon’s online Services: Carbon accounts, the account service at api.getcarbon.cc, online and shared playlists, public profiles, follows, listening history sync, Last.fm scrobbling through our service, listen-together rooms and the website at getcarbon.cc. It is part of the Terms of Service, and words like “Services” mean the same here as there.

It applies to anyone who uses the Services, including through a modified or third-party build of Carbon. It does not restrict what you do with the open-source app code, which the MIT license governs: reading, building, changing and sharing that code is welcome.

These rules are examples, not a complete list. If something is clearly harmful or illegal, it is not allowed here even if this page does not name it.

Content you share

Playlist names and details, your profile’s name and bio, and the name you show in a listen-together room can be seen by other people. They must not contain or link to:

  • anything illegal where you are or in the United States;
  • hate speech, or content that attacks or demeans people for their race, ethnicity, national origin, religion, caste, disability, disease, age, sex, gender identity or sexual orientation;
  • threats of violence, glorification of violent attacks, or support for terrorist or violent extremist groups;
  • harassment, bullying or intimidation of anyone;
  • sexually explicit content, or any sexual content involving minors (see Child safety);
  • someone else’s private information, such as home addresses, phone numbers, ID numbers or account details, without their permission;
  • content that encourages self-harm, suicide or eating disorders;
  • content that infringes copyright, trademarks or other rights;
  • scams, phishing, or deceptive links;
  • advertising or promotion that has nothing to do with the music, such as spam links or referral codes.

Playlist names that describe music in ordinary ways are fine, even when a song title or genre is explicit. These rules are about what you write and link to, not about the songs themselves.

How you treat people

  • No harassment. Don’t target, threaten, stalk or intimidate anyone, including through listen-together rooms, follows or our emails.
  • No impersonation. Don’t pretend to be another person, artist, organization or Carbon, and don’t misrepresent your connection to Carbon or to a music service.
  • No spam. Don’t use the Services to send unwanted messages, mass-create accounts or playlists, or trigger our emails to people who didn’t ask for them.
  • No fraud. Don’t use the Services to deceive people, collect their passwords or codes, or get money from them dishonestly.
  • Respect privacy. Don’t collect or share other people’s personal information from the Services without their permission.

Using the Services fairly

Carbon’s online services are shared by everyone who uses them. Don’t:

  • access, or try to access, accounts, sessions or data that are not yours;
  • guess, stuff or spray passwords, sign-in codes or backup codes, including with lists of credentials leaked from other sites;
  • probe, scan or test the Services for vulnerabilities without following our security reporting process, or exploit a vulnerability beyond what is needed to report it;
  • overload or disrupt the Services, for example with floods of requests or denial-of-service attacks;
  • get around rate limits, lockouts, suspensions, verification or any other limit, for example by rotating IP addresses or accounts;
  • scrape, crawl or bulk-collect data from the Services, including shared playlists and profiles, or use the account service through automated tools other than the Carbon app used as intended;
  • reverse engineer, decompile or try to extract the source code or secrets of the private, server-side parts of the Services, except where the law allows it despite this rule;
  • upload, link to or spread malware, or use the Services to attack anyone else;
  • sell, rent or share accounts, or resell access to the Services;
  • use the Services to build a competing hosted service, or to train or feed a product that copies them, without our permission.

Found a security problem? Report it privately as the Security page explains. Good-faith reports that follow that process are welcome and are not a breach of this policy.

Music services and copyright

Carbon plays music from services and sources you are entitled to use. Don’t use the Services, or any version of Carbon, to:

  • break the terms of Spotify, YouTube, Google or any other service you connect, including Spotify’s developer terms for your own developer app;
  • get around digital rights management, copy protection, regional limits or other technical measures that a music service or rights holder uses;
  • get Spotify Premium, YouTube Music or other paid features without paying for them;
  • download, rip, record or redistribute music, videos or lyrics from a service that does not allow it. Public-domain and Creative Commons recordings, and your own files, are fine;
  • share links to pirated music, or use your own server to distribute music you do not have the rights to share;
  • share your Spotify developer app or Client ID so others can get around Spotify’s limits.

Copyright complaints are handled under our Copyright and IP Policy.

Child safety

We have zero tolerance for child sexual abuse material (CSAM) and for any content or conduct that sexualizes, exploits or endangers children. This includes links to such material and attempts to groom or contact minors for sexual purposes.

When we find it, we remove it, close every account involved without warning, keep the information the law requires us to keep, and report it to the National Center for Missing & Exploited Children (NCMEC) and to law enforcement as the law requires. There is no appeal for these closures.

If you see anything like this on the Services, report it right away to support@getcarbon.cc. Please do not download, copy or forward the material itself, not even to us. Send the link or describe where it is. You can also report it directly to NCMEC’s CyberTipline, or to the police where you live.

What we do about it

We look into reports and problems we find ourselves. Depending on how serious it is and whether it has happened before, we may:

  • ask you to change something, or warn you;
  • make a playlist or profile private, or remove content;
  • end a listen-together room;
  • limit features, rate limit or block requests;
  • suspend your account, which signs it out everywhere and blocks sign-in until it is lifted;
  • close your account permanently and refuse new accounts from you;
  • report illegal activity to the authorities, or cooperate with lawful requests.

We may act without warning when the problem is serious, illegal, or puts people or the Services at risk. Where it is safe and lawful, we will tell you what we did and why.

Appeals

If you think we made a mistake, reply to our message or email support@getcarbon.cc with the subject “Appeal” and your account email. Tell us why you think the decision was wrong. We will look at it again and let you know the outcome. Records of suspensions are kept as the Privacy Policy describes.

Reporting a problem

To report content or behaviour that breaks this policy, email support@getcarbon.cc with:

  • the link to the shared playlist or profile, or the room code and time, or a description of where you saw it;
  • what the problem is and which rule it breaks;
  • your contact details, if you’d like to hear back.

For copyright, use the process in the Copyright and IP Policy. For security problems, report privately as the Security page explains. If someone is in immediate danger, contact your local emergency services first.

Changes to this policy

We may update this policy as Carbon changes, as the law requires, or as new kinds of misuse appear. We will post the new version here and update the date at the top. Material changes are announced as the Terms of Service describe.
