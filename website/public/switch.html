Switch to Carbon.

Your Spotify and YouTube Music libraries live in your accounts, so they appear when you sign in. Your own files are read from your folders. Playlists from anywhere else come in from files. Pick where you’re coming from.

From the Spotify app

Carbon plays Spotify itself, through Spotify’s official Web Playback SDK, so the Spotify app doesn’t need to be open. Spotify playback needs Premium and your own free Client ID.

Comes with you

  • Your playlists, Liked Songs and library. They’re in your Spotify account and appear after you sign in.
  • Your top artists, songs and albums over four weeks, six months or all time.
  • Playlists you own or collaborate on, as editable track lists when Spotify grants access.

Stays behind

  • Downloads. Carbon doesn’t save Spotify audio for offline listening.
  • Friend activity, Canvas, play counts, radio pages and Spotify’s own lyrics. Carbon has its own lyrics.
  • EQ and crossfade for Spotify songs. Carbon’s audio engine works on your own files.

From Spicetify

Spicetify modifies the official Spotify client. Carbon is a separate app with no Spotify app needed, so your Spotify library comes with you but Spicetify’s add-ons don’t. Several popular extensions have a built-in counterpart.

Comes with you

  • Your playlists, Liked Songs and library, after you sign in with Spotify.
  • Your top artists, songs and albums.

Stays behind

  • Themes, extensions, custom apps and snippets. None of them run in Carbon.
  • The Spotify-only features listed under The Spotify app, such as downloads and Canvas.

From Cider

Cider is an Apple Music client. Carbon doesn’t play Apple Music itself. It searches Apple Music’s public catalog, opens Apple’s official player, and brings your playlists across as Carbon playlists.

Comes with you

  • Your playlists, exported from Apple’s Music app. Carbon matches each song to a YouTube Music recording, and you review the matches before it saves the playlist.
  • Albums from the Apple Music catalog, imported the same way.

Stays behind

  • Apple Music in Carbon’s own player and queue. It plays only in Apple’s official player.
  • Anything set up inside Cider, such as themes.
  • Songs that don’t match. Carbon lists them so you can search by hand.

From YouTube Music desktop

For anyone using th-ch/youtube-music or ytmdesktop. Carbon plays YouTube Music beside Spotify and your own music, kept separate from Spotify, and builds in several things their plugins add.

Comes with you

  • Your library, playlists and Liked Music. They’re in your Google account and appear after you choose Sign in to YouTube Music in Carbon’s Settings.

Stays behind

  • Plugins. They don’t run in Carbon.
  • Ad blocking. Carbon plays through YouTube’s embedded player, a separate page it can’t reach into.
  • Downloads. Carbon doesn’t save YouTube Music audio.

From another player

Coming from foobar2000, MusicBee or your server’s web player? Carbon plays your files with its own audio engine, and signs in to your Navidrome, Jellyfin or Plex server.

Comes with you

  • Your music files. Carbon reads them from the folders you add.
  • Your ReplayGain tags and CUE sheets.
  • Your server’s library, with its own queue.
  • Playlists saved as files. Most players can save a playlist as M3U.

Stays behind

  • Plugins and add-ons from your old player.
  • Anything kept only in your old player’s database, such as play counts.
  • Exclusive-mode output. Carbon opens the audio device in shared mode.

First steps

  1. Download Carbon and install it.
  2. Create a free Carbon account. The first time you sign in on a device, we email you a code.
  3. For Spotify, make your own Client ID with the setup guide, then sign in with Spotify.
  4. For YouTube Music, choose Sign in to YouTube Music in Settings.
  5. Add your music folders under Local files, or connect your server in Settings.
  6. Bring other playlists in with Import playlist.
