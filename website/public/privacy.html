Privacy Policy

Last updated November 24, 2026

This policy is written in plain language. The short version: the Carbon app has no ads, no analytics and no tracking. This website counts visits as daily totals, without cookies, and asks before it remembers how you found it. Your Spotify sign-in, settings and listening stay on your device unless you choose to sync or share them. For a Carbon account we keep your email, a hashed password and the security records needed to protect it. We never sell your information, share it for advertising or use it to profile you, and you can see, export or delete it.

Who we are

Carbon is an independent project by pealz. Carbon decides how and why the information described here is used, so it is responsible for it (the “controller”, in GDPR terms). In this policy, “Carbon”, “we” and “us” mean that project and the person who runs it. Contact us about anything in this policy at support@getcarbon.cc.

This policy covers the Carbon desktop app for Windows, macOS and Linux, Carbon accounts and the account service at api.getcarbon.cc (including online playlists, shared playlists and profiles, listening history sync, Last.fm scrobbling and listen-together rooms), donations, and the website at getcarbon.cc. It sits alongside our Terms of Service.

Carbon is not affiliated with Spotify, Google or any other music service. What those services collect when you use them, including through Carbon, is covered by their own policies, such as Spotify’s privacy policy.

Account information

  • Your email address, whether you have verified it, and when you created your account.
  • Your password, stored only as a salted hash. We never store or see your password in readable form.
  • Your plan, which is Free for every account.

Security records

  • Short-lived request counters keyed to your IP address or email, used to rate-limit requests.
  • A security log of account events, such as sign-ups, sign-ins, failed sign-ins, lockouts, password changes and app authorizations, each with the time and the IP address it came from. We use it to lock accounts after repeated failed sign-ins and to detect fraud and abuse.
  • One-time codes and backup codes, stored only as hashes, along with how many attempts have been made.

Sessions and devices

For each browser or Carbon app signed in to your account, we keep a session record: whether it is a web or app session, when it was created and last used, and the IP address and browser or app identifier (user agent) it signed in from. You can see and end these sessions on your account page.

Playlists, profiles and history you sync

Public provider searches and playback contact the selected music service. Playlist matching sends the song and artist names you choose to YouTube Music. Downloaded audio and local file paths are stored on your device.

If you choose Save online, Carbon stores that playlist’s name, song identifiers and track metadata under your account. Online copies are private by default. Publishing a playlist or profile creates a link anyone with the link can open. You can make it private again, remove an online playlist, or delete your account to remove this data. Following a listener is saved under your account. If you turn on listening history sync, Carbon stores up to 5,000 Spotify or YouTube play timestamps and song metadata under your account. You can turn it off or delete your online history in Profile and sync.

Local file paths, audio files and the sign-in details of the services you connect are never included when you share.

Listen-together rooms

If you start or join a listen-together room, Carbon’s room service at api.getcarbon.cc passes the optional name the host enters, and what the host is playing (the song’s identifier, title, artists and length, the playback position and whether it is paused), to everyone in the room, so their apps can play along. People join with the room’s code.

Donations

If you donate, Stripe or GitHub processes your payment, and Carbon never sees your card number. From them we receive your name, your email address (GitHub shares it only if you allow it), the amount and the date of each donation, and we keep a record of any refund.

The Supporters wall on getcarbon.cc lists only people who opt in, with the name and optional link they choose. Ask us at any time and we will remove you.

Messages you send us

If you email us, we keep your message and our reply so we can help you.

Last.fm scrobbling

If you connect Last.fm in the updated desktop app and enable scrobbling, Carbon’s account service receives the artist, track, album, duration and play time. It signs and forwards each request to Last.fm. The service handles your Last.fm session key in memory and returns an encrypted connection to your device. It does not save scrobbles or Last.fm session keys in its database or application logs. Listening history sync is a separate setting.

Earlier versions sent plays directly to Last.fm. You must connect Last.fm again in Settings before the updated app sends a play through Carbon. You can pause scrobbling or disconnect Last.fm there at any time. Spotify and Last.fm handle their own passwords.

The Carbon app does not collect Discord credentials, analytics, telemetry or advertising identifiers. The website’s visit counts are described in Website visits.

What stays on your device

The Carbon app stores the following on your computer. It does not reach Carbon’s servers unless a feature described above sends it:

  • your Spotify sign-in tokens and your Carbon app sign-in token, stored in your system’s credential store, such as Windows Credential Manager or macOS Keychain;
  • your Spotify Client ID and app settings;
  • an encrypted Last.fm connection, if you set one up. The app presents it with your Carbon app session when submitting plays;
  • your ListenBrainz token, if you add one, in the system’s credential store;
  • the listening history and recap Carbon keeps for you, recordings you download and the paths of your local files.

You sign in to Spotify on Spotify’s own page, so Carbon never sees your Spotify password.

How we use information

  • To run your account: creating it, verifying your email, signing you in with two-step codes, keeping you signed in, and showing your sessions.
  • To provide the features you choose: syncing and sharing playlists, profiles and history, running listen-together rooms, and forwarding scrobbles.
  • To keep accounts secure: rate limiting, lockouts, detecting suspicious sign-ins and preventing abuse.
  • To enforce our terms: suspending accounts that seriously or repeatedly break the terms. A suspension signs the account out everywhere and blocks sign-in until it is lifted.
  • To improve the website: counting visits as daily totals, as described in Website visits.
  • To send account email: verification and sign-in codes, password reset codes, and important notices about your account or these policies. We do not send marketing email without your consent.
  • To handle donations: keeping records, handling refunds and cancellations, thanking you, and listing you on the Supporters wall if you opt in.
  • To help you: answering support requests.
  • To meet legal obligations: such as accounting records for donations, or responding to valid legal requests.

We do not use your information for advertising, and we do not build profiles of your listening.

Who we share it with

We do not sell your personal information or share it for cross-context behavioral advertising, and we do not show targeted ads. We share it only with:

  • Cloudflare, which hosts the website, the account service and its database (including the website’s visit counts), and protects them from attacks.
  • Resend, which delivers our account emails and so receives your email address and the content of those emails.
  • Stripe and GitHub, which process donations. You give your payment details to them directly, under their own privacy policies.
  • Authorities, when the law requires it, or when needed to protect the safety, rights or property of our users, the public or Carbon. See Requests from authorities.
  • A successor, if the project is transferred, in which case this policy continues to apply to your information unless you agree otherwise.

Our providers may use your information only to provide their services to us.

Some things are shared because you choose to share them. A playlist or profile you publish can be seen by anyone with its link. People in a listen-together room see the host’s name, if one was entered, and what the host is playing. The music services and scrobbling services you connect receive what the table in Services Carbon talks to shows; they are not our providers, and their own policies apply.

If your browser sends a Global Privacy Control signal, we treat it as a request to opt out of the sale or sharing of your information. We already do neither, and the website then sends no visit information at all.

Requests from authorities

We give information to governments, courts or law enforcement only when we must: in response to a valid, legally binding request such as a subpoena, court order or warrant, or when we believe in good faith that it is needed to prevent death or serious harm.

  • We check every request and push back on ones that are unclear, too broad or not legally valid.
  • We disclose only what the request requires. We hold little to begin with: no card numbers, no readable passwords and no listening history unless you sync it.
  • Unless the law or a court order forbids it, or it would put someone at risk, we tell the affected user before we disclose anything.

Cookies and browser storage

We use two cookies, and both are essential. Both are set by api.getcarbon.cc, are HttpOnly and Secure (page scripts can’t read them, and they only travel over HTTPS), and neither is used for analytics or advertising:

  • __Host-carbon_session keeps you signed in, for up to 30 days after your last visit and never more than 90 days after you signed in.
  • __Host-carbon_device remembers a browser for 90 days after you finish a sign-in there with an emailed code, so that browser can skip the code next time.

The website also keeps these small items in your browser:

  • in local storage: a flag noting that you have signed in before, so the site knows to check whether you still are; and your answer to the visit prompt (Allow or Reject);
  • in local storage, only if you choose Allow: where you first came from, as described in Website visits;
  • in session storage, deleted when you close the tab: your email address and sign-in step while you move between sign-up or sign-in pages, whether you have already seen the loading screen, and a ten-minute copy of Carbon’s public release information from GitHub.

The website is served by Cloudflare and loads its fonts from Google Fonts. Signed-in members download installers from Carbon’s website. Shared playlist pages load cover images from Spotify’s and YouTube’s image servers. Each of them receives standard request information such as your IP address and browser type.

The Cookies notice lists each item and how to change your choice.

Website visits

The getcarbon.cc website counts visits, so we can see which pages help and where people find Carbon. It uses no cookies and no third-party analytics for this. The Carbon app has no analytics at all.

When a page loads, your browser tells Carbon’s account service (api.getcarbon.cc) the page’s path, the address of the page that linked to it, and any campaign tags in the link (utm_source, utm_medium, utm_campaign). Clicking a download link also says which installer you picked. From the request itself, the service reads your device type (desktop, phone or tablet, from your browser’s user agent) and your country (from Cloudflare).

The service adds one to a daily counter for that combination and keeps nothing else. The linking address is cut down to its site name, such as reddit.com. Your IP address, your user agent and full addresses are never stored. A counter can’t be traced back to you or linked to your other visits. Counters are deleted after 400 days. Only Carbon’s admins can see them.

If you choose Allow

Your browser also keeps where you first came from (the linking address and campaign tags) in local storage. Later visits then count as returning, and a later download is credited to that first source. This record stays in your browser; only the site name and tags reach us, as above. Choose Reject, or change your answer with (also at the bottom of every page), and it’s deleted.

If your browser sends Global Privacy Control or Do Not Track, the website sends no visit information at all and doesn’t ask.

For visitors in the EEA, UK and Switzerland, the daily counts rely on our legitimate interest in running the website, and the first-visit record relies on your consent, which you can withdraw at any time.

How we protect it

  • Your password never leaves your device. Your browser turns it into a key with Argon2id, and we store only a keyed hash of that key (HMAC-SHA-256 with a separate server secret).
  • Signing in on a new browser requires a one-time code sent to your email; that browser is then remembered for 90 days. Codes expire after 10 minutes, allow 5 attempts and are stored hashed.
  • Ten failed sign-ins from one network pause sign-in for that email from that network for 30 minutes, and every endpoint is rate limited.
  • All traffic uses HTTPS with HSTS. Session cookies are HttpOnly, Secure and scoped to the account service, and state-changing requests are protected against cross-site request forgery.
  • Resetting or changing your password signs out your other sessions and app tokens.

The Security page has more detail and explains how to report a problem.

Security incidents

No system is perfectly secure. If we learn of a breach that affects your information, we will:

  • act quickly to contain it and find out what happened;
  • notify you without undue delay, by email or in the app, saying what happened, what information was involved, what we have done and what you can do;
  • notify any authority as the law requires, including a data protection authority within 72 hours where the GDPR or UK GDPR requires it;
  • keep a record of the incident and what we learned from it.

Your rights and choices

Wherever you live, you can:

  • Access your information: your account page shows your email, plan and sessions. Email us for a full copy.
  • Correct it: email us to change your email address.
  • Delete it: delete your account from your account page, or email us. You can also remove online playlists and your online history in the app.
  • Export it: email us and we will send your account data in a machine-readable format.
  • Object or restrict certain uses, where the law gives you that right.
  • Withdraw consent you gave, such as for the first-visit record or the Supporters wall, at any time. This does not affect what happened before.
  • Make shared content private again, or stop syncing history, in the app.

We will never treat you differently for exercising these rights.

Making a request

Send requests to support@getcarbon.cc from your account’s email address. We will respond within 30 days, or sooner if the law requires.

  • Checking it’s you. We may need to confirm it is you before acting, usually by confirming you control the account’s email address. We ask only for what we need to verify you, and use it only for that.
  • Agents. Someone you authorize may make a request for you. We may ask the agent for proof of authorization and ask you to confirm your identity.
  • Cost. Requests are free. If a request is clearly unfounded or excessive, we may refuse it or, where the law allows, charge a reasonable fee, and we will explain why.
  • Appeals. If we decline a request, in whole or in part, we will explain why. You can appeal by replying to our answer with the subject “Privacy appeal”. We will review the decision and tell you the result, with our reasons, within 45 days. If you are not satisfied, you can contact your state attorney general or your data protection authority.

US state privacy rights

California, Virginia, Colorado, Connecticut, Utah, Texas, Oregon and other US states with similar laws give residents rights over their personal information. Some of these laws may not apply to a small, free project like Carbon, but we offer these rights to every US resident:

  • to know what personal information we collect, use and disclose, and to get a copy of it in a portable format;
  • to correct it and to delete it;
  • to opt out of the sale of personal information, targeted advertising and profiling that has legal or similarly significant effects. We do none of these;
  • to limit the use of sensitive personal information. We use it only to sign you in and protect your account;
  • not to be discriminated against for using these rights;
  • to appeal if we decline a request, as described in Making a request.

California

This section, with What we collect and How long we keep it, is our notice at collection under the California Consumer Privacy Act, as amended by the CPRA. Here is how this policy maps to California’s categories over the past 12 months:

  • Identifiers: name (if you donate), email address, IP address, user agent, account and session identifiers.
  • Commercial information: donation records (the amount and date), if you donate.
  • Internet activity: security records of account requests and, if you turn on sync, listening history.
  • Content you provide: online playlists, your profile’s name and bio, who you follow, and the name you show in a room.
  • Sensitive personal information: account login credentials (email and password), used only to sign you in and protect your account.

We collect these from you and your devices, use them for the purposes in How we use information, keep them for the periods in How long we keep it, and disclose them only to the service providers in Who we share it with. We do not sell or share personal information, we have not done so in the past 12 months, and we do not use sensitive personal information to infer characteristics about you. We offer no financial incentives: donations unlock nothing.

You have the right to know, access, correct and delete your personal information, and not to be discriminated against for using these rights. You may use an authorized agent; we may ask the agent for proof of authorization and ask you to confirm your identity. We respond to verified requests within 45 days.

We do not share personal information with third parties for their own direct marketing, so California’s “Shine the Light” law gives us nothing to disclose.

EEA, UK and Swiss residents

The legal basis for each use is in How we use information. In short, we rely on these legal bases:

  • Contract: to provide your account and the features you choose.
  • Legitimate interests: to keep accounts and the Services secure, prevent abuse and count website visits, balanced against your rights.
  • Legal obligation: to keep donation records for accounting and respond to lawful requests.
  • Consent: where we ask for it, such as listing you on the Supporters wall or remembering where you first came from, which you can withdraw at any time.

You have the right to access, rectify, erase, restrict and port your data, to object to processing based on legitimate interests, and to withdraw consent. Where we rely on legitimate interests, you can ask us how we balanced them against your rights.

You may also complain to a data protection authority: in the EEA, the authority where you live, work or think a breach happened; in the UK, the Information Commissioner’s Office; in Switzerland, the Federal Data Protection and Information Commissioner. We would appreciate the chance to sort it out with you first.

Automated decisions

We do not make decisions about you based solely on automated processing that have legal or similarly significant effects, and we do not profile you. Some security rules run automatically, such as rate limits, expiring codes and the 30-minute pause after ten failed sign-ins. They are temporary and exist only to protect accounts. If one blocks you by mistake, email us and we will look at it.

Children

Carbon is not intended for children under 13, and we do not knowingly collect personal information from them, in line with the US Children’s Online Privacy Protection Act (COPPA). Where the law sets a higher age of digital consent, younger users need a parent or guardian’s consent, and users under 18 need a parent or guardian’s consent under our terms.

If we learn that we have collected personal information from a child under 13, we will delete it and close the account. If you believe a child under 13 has created an account, or you are a parent who wants to review or delete a child’s information, contact us and we will act promptly.

International transfers

Carbon is run from the United States, and our providers operate globally. Your information may be processed in the United States and other countries whose data protection laws differ from yours. Where required, we rely on safeguards such as the European Commission’s Standard Contractual Clauses offered by our providers, with the UK and Swiss additions where they apply.

Changes to this policy

We will post any update here and change the date at the top. If a change materially affects how we handle your information, we will tell you by email or in the app before it takes effect.

Contact

Questions or requests about your privacy: support@getcarbon.cc. Security problems: see Security. Every legal document is listed in the Legal center.
